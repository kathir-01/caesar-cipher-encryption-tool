What is the Caesar Cipher?

The Caesar Cipher is one of the oldest and simplest known encryption techniques. It is a type of substitution cipher in which each letter in the plaintext is shifted by a fixed number of positions down the alphabet.

For example, with a shift of 3, letter A becomes D, B becomes E, and Z wraps around to C.

📜

Historical Origin

Named after Julius Caesar, who used it with a shift of 3 to protect military communications in the 1st century BCE. Roman historian Suetonius recorded that Caesar used it to send confidential dispatches to his generals Cicero and Mark Antony.

📐

Mathematical Foundations

The encryption can be represented using Modular Arithmetic over integers modulo 26:

Encryption: Ek(x) = (x + k) mod 26

Decryption: Dk(x) = (x - k) mod 26

Where x is the 0-indexed position of the letter (A=0, B=1, ... Z=25) and k is the key shift (1–25).

🔓

Cryptanalysis & Vulnerabilities

The Caesar Cipher is broken easily using two primary techniques:

  • Brute-Force Attack: Trying all 25 possible shifts since the keyspace is extremely small.
  • Frequency Analysis: Comparing letter frequencies in the ciphertext against standard English letter frequencies (e.g. 'E' occurs ~12.7% of the time).
💻

ASCII & Unicode Handling

Our tool preserves all character formatting:

  • Uppercase Letters (ASCII 65–90): Shifted independently within A–Z bounds.
  • Lowercase Letters (ASCII 97–122): Shifted independently within a–z bounds.
  • Numbers & Symbols: Preserved in exact position without alteration for maximum readability.
🚀

Modern Cryptography Evolution

While obsolete for securing sensitive data today, the Caesar Cipher forms the foundational teaching model for modern ciphers like the Vigenère Cipher, Enigma Machine, and modern symmetric algorithms like AES-256.
